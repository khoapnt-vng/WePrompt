Two rows, one kind

Two rows, not one row with a column. They differ in six of seven columns, and a single row would make every reader work out which half applies before they could read it. The discriminator already exists and costs nothing: IMPORTED and GENERATING are statuses on the fixed list, so provenance is already a state the block holds. This is the same shape as motion's two measures for one member versus a set: one kind, two behaviours, stated separately so neither has to be qualified.

The thing that makes sound different, stated once so every answer below follows from it

Every other kind's subject is an image. A sound block's subject is the words — the line it was made from, or the filename it arrived as. The waveform is not the artifact; it is a decoration that has never told anyone anything they needed. That single claim answers shedding, density, SLATE, and what replaces the thumbnail: nothing replaces it, because it was never the subject.

§1
The level — one kind owning an attribute about another

You are right that this is the first one, and it will not be the last, so it should be answered as a rule rather than as a cell. The level is not a property of the sound and not a property of the film: it is a property of the film's use of the sound. That distinction already has a precedent in this grammar — chain position is a property of a shot's place in a Beat, not of the clip.

The rule, generally

When one kind owns an attribute about another: the owner carries the control; the subject carries the fact. The cut row gets the level as an on-block action — it is free and reversible, so my own placement rule allows nowhere else. The sound row states it as plain text and offers no control: −6 dB under #the_cut. Never two sliders, never a mirrored control, never a disabled one.

Why not the rail

A level costs nothing, changes nothing downstream, and is undone by dragging it back. Sending it to the rail would make the cheapest act in the product require a sentence, and would put a mixing decision in the same queue as a $40 render. The rail keeps only where it sits under the cut — which beats it plays under — because that is a structural claim about the film.

And what the fact buys

A person who finds a sound block on a forty-block canvas learns from one line that it is in the film, where, and how loud — without opening the cut. That is the whole reason the fact is stated rather than left implicit: a block that says nothing about its use looks unused.

§3
The four things you said we would otherwise get wrong

QUESTION
ANSWER

Is a re-mix local RENDERING?
It is — but it never happens to a sound block. Mixing is the cut's operation: the sound is an input, unchanged, and what is being composed is the film. So RENDERING appears on the cut and GENERATING on the sound, and the two never appear on the same block. That keeps the refund distinction exactly where it is legible: a person who cancels something on a sound block gets money back, and there is no control on a sound block that could ever imply otherwise.

What does SLATE mean for audio?
Nothing — audio has no SLATE, and that is not an omission. A slate holds time because picture is the film's clock: shot seconds are the only length anyone sets, and the Beat sums them. Audio takes its length from what it sits under, or from its own words. So unrendered audio holds nothing; it is simply not there.Which answers the second half: a film missing picture and a film missing sound are different conditions and must not share a word. Missing picture is a hole — PARTIAL, with slates in the filmstrip holding their time. Missing sound is a film that plays, silent, at full length. PARTIAL therefore counts picture only, and an audio shortfall is stated as fact in the cut's audio lane — "narration for Beat 2 — not generated" — never as a block-level status. A film is not incomplete because nobody has recorded a voice yet.

Do audio members carry chain position?
No, and you are right that the absence is load-bearing. Chain position exists for one physical reason: each shot starts on the last frame of the one before, so a shot's neighbours are its inputs. Nothing conditions a narration line except the words. Audio instead carries a time anchor — the Beat it starts under — which looks similar and behaves oppositely.Three consequences worth writing down, because they all follow from the absence: reordering audio is always free (there is no chain to rewrite); audio never goes STALE from a reorder, only from a script edit; and audio is therefore outside Ruling 1b entirely — the priced-and-warned intra-Beat case cannot arise for it. If a future audio route ever conditions the next line on the previous one, that invariant breaks and this row must be reopened.

Does a narration quote state the same three numbers?
Two facts and one estimate, and no rate. The quote states the price and the billed unit — characters, because that is what the provider charges and a person editing the line can see it move. Duration is shown as an estimate, labelled as one, because the length of a read does not exist until it is read.Price-per-second is omitted deliberately. A rate computed over an estimate is a made-up number wearing the clothes of a fact, and the quote rule says the price is never approximated. A film's quote can carry a rate because its duration is set by the person; a read's cannot.

§4
Density and shedding for a thing you cannot see

Your framing was that a quietened sound block is "a row of text about a thing you cannot perceive". That is the right worry and the wrong conclusion: a row of text is what a sound block should be at every density. Nothing replaces the thumbnail, because the words were always the subject and the waveform was always the decoration.

The floor

At its smallest — canvas 640, nine or more blocks — a sound block is one header line and one action: handle, duration, play, and the line. That is a complete sound block, not a degraded one. Compare a stills set at the same size, which is by then two rows of thumbnails and a "+12 more": sound is the only kind that loses nothing on the way down.

No ceiling either

At 1–3 blocks, every other kind grows and centres. Sound does not. A lone narration block at 900px of composed width is a sentence in a very large box, and generous density would make it look like the canvas expected something more to arrive. It stays 4 of 12 and sits where it falls. This is the one kind exempt from the generous step.

One rule nobody asked for, which sound needs and no other kind does

Playback is exclusive and never automatic. Starting any sound stops every other sound and the cut; nothing on the canvas ever plays unbidden. Pictures can be looked at forty at a time; audio cannot be heard two at a time, so the canvas must enforce what the eye enforces for free. Without this rule, a person scrubbing a clip while a music bed plays hears a mix that is not in the film.

What the waveform is still for

One thing: a scrub target while something is playing. So it is present at the default density and while playing, absent otherwise — the same logic as region 4, which exists only while it has work to do. It is never a thumbnail, never a static identity for the block, and never the thing that survives when space runs out.

§5
D2 — what a route that cannot speak looks like

Before: the block is never offered

Where silentOutput is true for the bound route, generated sound does not appear as a possibility anywhere on the canvas — no control, no empty block, no greyed row. Asking for it gets a sentence from the Director: "this binding can't produce audio." The grammar already covers this and needs no new clause: a control that would fail is omitted, never disabled. D2 and that rule are the same rule.

After: an existing block outlives its route

A rendered narration whose route later loses audio stays exactly as it is — it is an artifact and artifacts do not degrade. What disappears is Retry, and re-generation stops being available in the rail. The block does not announce this; nothing has happened to it. A person who asks for a new read is told why, once, in words.

The case D2 half-anticipates: it ran, and returned silence

"If a model produces silence, the product says so and moves on." That needs no new status, because Ruling 2a already made FAILED carry a reason and a cost-truth: FAILED · returned silence · spent. It is the only failure in the product that costs money and produces a file, and the cost-truth is the only reason a person can tell it apart from a network failure. Retry is absent here — retrying a route that answered with silence spends again for the same answer.

§6
The fixed list — two inferences confirmed, one thing to add

† READY TO RENDER — confirmed, and scoped

Member level, and narrower than the list implies: it appears only where a member could start and a person has not said go — CS3's "start frame · ready to render · 6s". It is not a resting state and never appears on a member nobody has asked for. Distinct from QUEUED by one fact: no money is committed.

† DRAFTED — confirmed, and kind-local

Block level, document only, paired with CURRENT. It should be marked kind-local on the list rather than sitting in the general block-level set, or it will be implemented as available to six kinds and used by one.

The list needs one more distinction, and it is not a word

STALE appears at both levels with one meaning — "plays, but off the chain" — which is a chain fact. Audio has no chain and still stales, from a script edit. Same word, two causes, and the pairing differs: chain-stale offers Re-render chain / Keep; words-stale offers only Keep, because there is no chain to re-render. One word, two action sets — worth a note on the list so nobody implements the chain pairing globally.

Three decisions here to argue with

1. The waveform sheds first and entirely. It is the most visible thing in a sound block and I am calling it decoration.2. PARTIAL counts picture only, so a film with no sound at all reads as whole. Defensible, and it will surprise someone.3. Exclusive playback stops the cut when a person plays a sound block. Correct for hearing, mildly rude in use.
