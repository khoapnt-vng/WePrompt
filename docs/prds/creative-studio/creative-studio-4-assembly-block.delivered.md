An Assembly is a cut, and it holds no pictures

No new kind. Everything below is the cut row of grammar v2 at real size — the film composition over other blocks, owning a timeline of references it does not contain. Drawn because three things in that row have never been seen together: slates holding their time, an audio lane whose absences are stated rather than gapped, and an order that is legible without a single drag handle.

The header says what plays

0:48 of 1:15. Not "64% complete", not a bar. The second number is the film's length including everything unmade, because slates hold their time — so the film's length is known from the moment the script is, and it does not creep upward as work lands.

No Download, and no disabled Download

An export of a film with four slates in it would either fail or lie. The control is absent until the film is whole, per the grammar's rule that a control which would fail is omitted rather than greyed.

Level sits here, and nowhere else

The music bed's level is a property of the film's use of the sound, so the control is on the cut and the sound block states it as a fact. Owner carries the control; subject carries the fact.

One consequence line, never a paragraph

"Four shots hold as slates · 0:27 unmade" is the whole of what this block has to say about its own incompleteness. Producing them is speech to the Director, so no button for it appears here.

§2
The filmstrip's five states, at 22px

Segment width is duration, always — which is why a slate cannot be hidden: it occupies exactly the time it will occupy. Colour never carries meaning alone; every state has a texture or a border, so the strip survives being 22px tall on a bad monitor.

Rendered

Solid. The only state that needs no explanation, and therefore the only one with no label.

Slate

Holds its time, no render yet. Grey hatch, no colour — an unmade shot is not a problem, it is a plan.

Stale

Same hatch, warm. It plays, but off the chain — so it is drawn as present, not as missing.

Failed

Rules out of the strip alone: the reason and the cost-truth live on the Beat's own block, never here. The strip says where, not why.

Queued

Dotted outline: committed, nothing happening. It becomes solid without an animation — the strip never pulses.

What never appears in the strip

Thumbnails. A 22px filmstrip of tiny pictures is decoration that costs a fetch per shot and answers nothing the beat labels do not. The strip's job is proportion and state.

§3
The audio lane — where absence is a sentence, not a hole

#harbour_morning · −6 dB, ducked under the whole film

#hands_narration · 0:12 · anchored to Beat 02

not generated

Narration for Beat 04 — not generated. The film plays silent there, at full length.

Audio has a time anchor, not a chain position

"Anchored to Beat 02" looks like a chain position and behaves oppositely: nothing conditions a read except its words. So audio segments may be moved freely, never go stale from a move, and sit outside Ruling 1b entirely.

Why the missing narration is drawn dashed and also written out

A dashed box alone reads as a hole in the film, which it is not — the picture is complete there. The sentence is what stops a person from thinking the film is broken. This is the one place in the grammar where a drawing needs a caption to be honest.

And it does not make the block PARTIAL

PARTIAL counts picture only. A film with no sound at all reads as whole, and I stand by it — but it is the decision in this drawing most likely to surprise someone in Pilot.

§4
Ordering, drawn — a selected segment, and no handles anywhere

This is the part the commission could not settle on paper. Selecting a segment reveals an inspector row inside the block — the only thing in the product that states chain position, cost and consequence in one place. It appears on click or on keyboard focus and takes one line.

Off the chain since 1.2 was re-rendered. It plays. Alt+↑/↓ moves it — inside Beat 01 that rewrites the chain and costs $2.40; across Beats it is free.

Why the cost is in the button and not in a dialog

Re-render chain · $2.40 beside a plain Keep. A person deciding between two named outcomes with one price visible does not need a confirmation step, and Keep being a real option is what makes this a consequence rather than a gate.

The one line that teaches the whole ordering rule

"Inside Beat 01 that rewrites the chain and costs $2.40; across Beats it is free." Ruling 1b, said once, in the only place a person is about to act on it. Nobody has to learn a cost model in the abstract.

Selection dims, it does not hide

Unselected Beats drop to 55%. Proportion stays readable — you can still see that the selected shot is five seconds of a seventy-five second film, which is most of what you need to judge whether re-rendering it matters.

And still no drag handle

Nothing here is draggable, at any state, ever. The inspector row is the human path BUG-174 permits: keyboard-reachable, present only while a member is selected, and never permanent per-row chrome. The Director's typed reorder remains primary and deterministic.

§7
What this block does not contain, and what to argue with

Six things deliberately absent

A transport bar · a timeline zoom · thumbnails in the strip · a drag handle · a Download until whole · an empty state. The last one matters most: this block cannot exist empty, because a film with no shots is a script, and a script is a different kind.

It contains no pictures at all

Every segment is a reference to a member of another block. Nothing here is the artifact; the player is a view onto other blocks' work. That is the whole reason a cut is a separate kind from motion, and it is why re-cutting is free.

Three decisions in this drawing to argue with

1. No thumbnails in the filmstrip. It is the most expected thing in a film UI and I have left it out.2. The price rides in the button (Re-render chain · $2.40) with no confirmation step.3. Two audio rows are drawn as fixed lanes. Beyond three or four generated cues this stops scaling, and the honest answer then is a count, not more lanes.

Still open, and not solved here

The bin has a rule and no surface — it is the one canvas-level thing the grammar names and never draws. Everything on this page is block-level, so it could not be answered here.
