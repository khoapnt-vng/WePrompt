# Creative Studio 4 — a commission: three gaps the grammar leaves open

## The short version

The block grammar you delivered is accepted and is what Phase 1 is built against. Phase 6
adds Assembly and film, and in preparing for it we found **three things the grammar does not
govern**. None of them is a new kind and none of them needs a drawing. All three are rows or
rules missing from a table you have already written.

We are asking for one more pass over the grammar, not a new one.

## What changed since the last commission

Phase 5 completed the clean cutover: the four views are gone and the canvas is the only
workspace. The bounded two-Piece visual conditioning shipped, so a photograph can now be made
using up to two existing photographs as references.

Phase 6 is next: **Assembly** (an ordered arrangement of Pieces into a film), the semantic
reference workflow, and video, sound and voice.

Preparing that scope, we checked every surface against your grammar and the earlier CS2/CS3
work. Most of Phase 6 is already covered — your ten-kinds-are-six table spans `video`,
`sound`, `cut`, `clip set`, `character set` and `place set`; cast and places are settled as
stills with captions; project style is settled as Brief prose. **We are not reopening any of
that.** Three things fell through.

## Why a table pass, and not more drawings

Same reason as last time. Every one of these is a question about which rule applies where,
not about what something looks like. If we ask for pictures we will get three good pictures
and still not know the rule, and we will get it wrong the next eight times.

## What we are asking you to specify

### 1. A row for the quote block

The quote block appears on the canvas and has **no row in the grammar** — the word does not
occur in the delivered spec. The canvas design defines it as:

> "a provisional renderer presentation of quoted intent. It is not a Piece and is not part of
> canvas inventory."

So it is the one thing a person sees on the canvas that your table does not govern: not a
kind, not inventory, but present, and carrying the single most consequential number in the
product.

Phase 6 sharpens this. A video quote carries a duration and a cost an order of magnitude
above a photograph's, and an Assembly can hold several quotes at once.

Please give it the same columns as every other row — header, statuses it can hold, actions on
the block versus in the rail, measure, and both rail extremes — **or** tell us it is not a row
at all and give us the rule that governs it instead. Either answer is fine; the absence is
not.

The specific thing we will otherwise get wrong: whether a quote sheds under density like a
block does, or is exempt because a cost may never be quietened. Our reading of your own
invariant — density may quieten satisfied work, never a decision — says exempt, but we would
rather have it from you than infer it.

### 2. Assembly ordering against the canvas's own order

These two rules meet on one canvas and currently contradict:

- The canvas is **"one automatically laid-out, dependency-ordered board"** with **no persisted
  coordinates and no hand reordering** (Phase 1 plan). That is settled and we are not
  reopening it.
- An **Assembly is a hand-ordered arrangement by definition.** Its whole content is an order a
  person or the Director chose.

So a canvas that forbids hand ordering must host a block whose entire meaning is a hand-chosen
order. We need the rule that reconciles them — most likely that ordering *inside* an Assembly
block is a different act from ordering blocks *on* the canvas, and that the block is the
boundary. If that is right, say so and say what marks the boundary visually, because a person
who can drag inside one block and not between blocks will otherwise learn the canvas is
inconsistent.

**This question depends on a ruling we are settling separately** — whether reordering is
Director-owned, human, or both, and which reorderings are free. You will get that answer with
this commission. Please treat the cost model as given rather than proposing one; the
constraint comes from beat-scoped chaining, not from interaction design.

We flag one thing for you, because it is a contradiction inside your own delivered table: the
set/storyboard row places **`reorder members`** on the block, while the `cut` row places
**"reordering the film; hard cuts and joins"** in the rail. Under your stated placement rule —
free and reversible on the block, everything else in the rail — those two cannot both be
right. The ruling will fix the cost model; we would like your table corrected to match it.

### 3. Proposal review at film scale

You ruled that **a proposal is a status, not a component**, and we accepted it. It is the
cheapest guarantee of one home and we still think it is right.

At film scale it meets a case Pilot 1 never produced: **one proposal that spans or creates many
blocks at once** — "storyboard these eight Beats", which proposes eight blocks that do not yet
exist. Against the one-pending rule, we need to know:

- Does a multi-block proposal render as `PROPOSED` on every affected block, on a single
  representative one, or somewhere else entirely?
- What does `PROPOSED` look like on a block that **does not exist yet** — a proposed block is
  not a satisfied one and cannot be quietened, but it is also not work in progress.
- Does one pending proposal spanning eight blocks consume the one-pending slot for all eight,
  or for the whole canvas? (We believe the whole canvas, but this is exactly the decision we
  will otherwise get wrong.)
- Where does the judgement footer live when the judgement covers many blocks? Your rule puts
  it on the block; eight footers for one decision is clearly wrong.

## Constraints

- **The six kinds are fixed.** Nothing here adds a kind. If your answer needs one, say so
  explicitly and tell us what it merges with.
- **The status vocabulary is being reconciled separately** and you will get a fixed list. Do
  not design around the current names — `RUNNING`, in particular, ships as "Generating" and
  "Rendering" and will change.
- **Density steps, six regions and the twelve-column measure stand as delivered.**
- Everything resolves to Arco components, and every string ships in twelve locales, so a label
  that only works in English is a label we cannot use.

## What we are not asking

- **Not the six kinds again.** Ten-kinds-are-six is settled and Phase 1 is built on it.
- **Not References, cast, places or style.** Settled: cast and places are stills with
  captions; project style is Brief prose, not a reference kind. We checked specifically and
  found the rulings already exist.
- **Not what an Assembly orders.** Your `cut` kind already settles it — "the film composition
  over other blocks… owns a timeline of references it does not contain" — with the filmstrip at
  one segment per Beat.
- **Not sound or voice.** CS3 already carries decisions and slices for narration, voice and
  SFX.
- **Not drawn screens, for any of the three.** Rows and rules.
